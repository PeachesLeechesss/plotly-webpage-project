A tiny observatory · interactive data story

What does a longer life look like?

Explore the relationship between national income and life expectancy across the world. Each bubble is a country. Size represents population; colour represents continent. Use the play button or slider to watch the story unfold.

Created: 27 October 2026

Income, health, and population over time

Hover over a bubble for country details. The horizontal axis is logarithmic to compare lower- and higher-income countries.

How to read the picture

01 · The upward drift
Higher GDP per capita often accompanies higher life expectancy, but income is only one part of the health puzzle.
02 · Different paths
Continents overlap, yet regional trajectories reflect different histories and public-health conditions.
03 · Bubble size
Large bubbles represent more people, making populous countries easy to spot.
04 · Time changes the view
Use the controls to compare country trajectories from 1952 to 2007.

A note on interpretation

This is an exploratory country-level visualization, not a causal analysis. A relationship between national income and life expectancy does not show that income alone causes a longer life.

Data: Gapminder country-level data distributed in the R package gapminder. R Markdown source is included in this project.
